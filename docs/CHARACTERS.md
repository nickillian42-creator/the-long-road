# Character Bible — THE LONG ROAD
Campaign canon locked by Nic (2026-10-05). This is the authoritative reference for the two protagonist routes, their spouses, and the rules governing them. Revisit only with Nic's approval.

## Permanent rule
**THE PLAYER SHOULD KNOW ONLY WHAT THE CREW KNOWS.**
Inventory names, descriptions, quests, dialogue, and UI must never reveal information the crew has not discovered. This applies to CUSTODIAN, locations, characters, and quest language — not just items.

## The two routes (v1)
There are two protagonist routes. They are **not** pronoun swaps. Each route receives occasional contextual solutions the other does not — Jack physical/strength solutions, Evelyn insight/improvisation solutions. Neither route is mechanically superior overall.

The surname **Mercer** is canonical and fixed: surname-recognition breadcrumbs (a CUSTODIAN system reacting to it, an NPC pausing — *"There was another Mercer"*) are authored against it.

## MALE ROUTE — Jack & Claire

### Jack Mercer (player character)
- **Age:** 37. **Height:** 6'4".
- **Core traits:** Brave, physically powerful, protective, loyal, stubborn, calm under pressure.
- Jack grew up in Mercy. He is the man people call when something heavy needs moving, something dangerous needs confronting, or somebody needs carrying home.
- His size is immediately noticeable, and his strength matters in gameplay: forcing doors, moving wreckage, carrying wounded people, intimidating aggressors — occasionally accomplishing things the female protagonist cannot accomplish the same way.
- He is not a meathead. He is emotionally perceptive, does not enjoy violence, and has a surprisingly gentle relationship with Claire.
- **Defining flaw:** he has trouble letting go. Sometimes that is beautiful. Sometimes it is dangerous. Claire understands that better than anyone.

### Claire Mercer (Jack's wife)
- **Age:** 35.
- **Core traits:** Intelligent, resourceful, stubborn, curious, compassionate, mechanically capable.
- Claire is not waiting in Colorado to be rescued. She has survived there.
- Before disappearing, she was one of Mercy's best people at understanding old-world electronics and communications equipment. She could make dead technology work just long enough to learn something from it. That is ultimately what took her away.

## FEMALE ROUTE — Evelyn & Daniel

### Evelyn Mercer (player character)
- **Age:** 35.
- **Core traits:** Brave, highly intelligent, observant, resourceful, determined, excellent at reading people.
- Evelyn is far from physically helpless, but her signature advantage is figuring things out. Where Jack sees a locked door, Evelyn notices the emergency release cable behind the wall panel. Where Jack intimidates someone into talking, Evelyn realizes they are lying.
- Her intelligence creates *different solutions*, not "+10 Intelligence" dialogue.
- **Defining flaw:** she believes every problem has a solution if she thinks long enough. Eventually the road presents situations where there simply isn't a perfect answer.

### Daniel Mercer (Evelyn's husband)
- **Age:** 37.
- **Core traits:** Tough, compassionate, practical, courageous, mechanically inclined.
- Daniel was one of Mercy's strongest scouts. He knew vehicles, routes, and survival outside the settlement better than most.
- He volunteered for the expedition because he believed Mercy couldn't survive forever by hiding from the world.
- Like Claire, he is not sitting somewhere waiting. He has been fighting his own battle the entire time.

## The marriage
Jack/Claire and Evelyn/Daniel are **genuinely happy marriages**. No secret-hatred reveal. They tease each other, argue, know each other's annoying habits, have had hard years — and love each other. Their unresolved conflict comes from the expedition.

## Why the spouse left Mercy
About **18 months before the game begins**, Mercy's long-range receiver picked up something unusual — not Station Seven's transmission. A weak, repeating machine signal from the northwest. Most dismissed it as an old automated system. Claire/Daniel didn't: **the signal occasionally changed. Something was still operating it.**

Mercy's water and electrical systems were already deteriorating. The council authorized a small expedition to investigate old infrastructure and determine whether the signal might lead to equipment that could help Mercy.

**The council permitted only one member from each household** — the expedition was dangerous, and Mercy could not afford to lose entire families. Claire/Daniel volunteered first. Jack/Evelyn wanted to go too, fought the decision, and ultimately stayed.

Their last serious argument: the protagonist said *"Don't go."* The spouse answered: *"If nobody goes, eventually there won't be a Mercy left to come back to."* They reconciled before departure — but not completely.

## What happened to the expedition (unknown to the player initially)
The expedition followed the signal and eventually encountered infrastructure connected to **CUSTODIAN**. Something went badly wrong. The expedition fragmented. People died. Some disappeared. Claire/Daniel survived.

Over the following 18 months, they slowly learned the signal wasn't simply a beacon — something was trying to communicate across parts of the old continuity network. Eventually that trail led toward Colorado. Toward Station Seven.

## Why they didn't come home
They tried. At some point returning became more dangerous than continuing: routes closed, people hunted them, equipment failed. And eventually the spouse discovered something that changed the equation — **Mercy may be connected to whatever CUSTODIAN is doing. Going home blindly could lead danger directly to everyone there.** So Claire/Daniel kept moving: trying to understand what they had found, trying to find a way to warn Mercy.

## The transmission break-in (campaign inciting incident)
The spouse eventually reaches — or gains access to — the Station Seven communications infrastructure. A transmission is already being prepared. They realize it can reach Mercy. **They interfere with it.** That is why their voice appears in the broadcast:

*"If this reaches Mercy… tell them I'm alive."*

Then:

*"And if you're hearing this—"*

— before the signal is lost.

### The two-voice rule (permanent)
The Station Seven broadcast voice and the spouse's break-in are **two separate voices**. Josh finds something about the Station voice familiar (he heard it sixty years ago). The protagonist **instantly recognizes** Claire/Daniel. The writing must never let the player confuse them: the Station voice is calm and steady; the spouse's voice is raw, fragmenting, emotional.

## Breadcrumbs (across the 780 miles)
Not every chapter; never conveniently spaced. Instead: someone remembers them. A campsite holds something recognizable. A trader carries an object of theirs. A name appears somewhere impossible. A radio recording. A CUSTODIAN system reacts to the surname Mercer. Eventually someone says *"Mercer?"* — pause — *"There was another Mercer."*

## The reunion (final quarter)
The player genuinely finds their spouse. The spouse does not recognize them at first — the protagonist is exhausted, filthy, weathered. Then recognition. No speech. No dialogue wheel. No exposition.

Claire: *"Jack?"* — or — Daniel: *"Evelyn?"*

They cross the distance. They embrace. Give it time.

*"You came."*
*"You knew I would."*
*"I hoped."*

## After the reunion (final act)
The spouse does **not** become an NPC standing in a room. For the final act, **they join the traveling party**. They have opinions about what the player has done. They meet Mara, Eli, and Josh. They recognize NPCs from their own journey. They know things about CUSTODIAN the player doesn't. They can disagree with the player. The last hours become about something different: *you crossed the world to find each other — now decide what you're going to do together.*

## Spouse survival rule (locked)
Reunion is **guaranteed** once the player reaches that story point. The spouse must **never** be automatically killed shortly after reunion. However, later player decisions in the final act *may* create outcomes in which Claire/Daniel dies — such an outcome must be causally connected to player choices, never a cheap scripted twist.

## Route differentiation rule (locked)
Certain situations genuinely change by route. Jack might get **[STRENGTH — lift the collapsed beam]**; Evelyn might instead get **[INSIGHT — the support isn't carrying the weight; release the brace]**. Same problem, different person, sometimes different consequences. Choosing a route has value beyond portraits and pronouns. Neither route is superior.
